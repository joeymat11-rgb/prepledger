# You started a recomp this week. Nobody said so out loud.

The trend reads 163.4 and the rate reads 0.97 lb a week — 0.59% of bodyweight,
right in the middle of the range that protects lean. On the face of it, a good
week. But that rate is measured over fourteen days, and half of those days
belong to a version of this cut that stopped existing on Wednesday.

Here is what actually happened. Your last four logged days ran 2,800 / 2,800 /
3,000 / 2,900 — an average of **2,875**. Your measured maintenance is somewhere
around **2,850–3,050**: the engine fits 2,911, your app fits 2,854 with a
ceiling of 3,039, and doing the arithmetic straight off the last fortnight's
loss lands at about 3,040. Pick any of them and four days at 2,875 is a deficit
of roughly nothing.

At the same time you added a set to **eight muscle groups in three days** —
hams, chest and rear delt on Friday, then quads, abs, biceps, triceps and back
this morning. Protein is 190–200 a day. Friday's leg session is the best one in
the file: extension 155 × 8,9, hack 160 × 12,11,13, ham 120 × 11,9,11.

Eating at maintenance, adding volume, protein high, training well — that is not
a cut. That is a recomp, and it is a perfectly good block to run at your body
fat. The problem is that you drifted into it rather than deciding it, and the
app is still running cut machinery around you: EASE 1, a rate band written in
pounds, a trend gauge waiting for a loss that this week's food will not produce.

## The three things that matter this week

**1. The deficit is gone, and the gauge hasn't noticed yet.** The 0.97 lb/wk on
your screen is real arithmetic over stale days. As this week fills the window I
expect the measured rate to fall under 0.7 lb/wk by around the 16th, and under
your band's own floor of 0.8. That is not a failure — it is what eating at
maintenance does — but it will look like a stall on a gauge built for a cut, and
I would rather you see it coming than read it as a plateau. (Observation only:
the app owns your intake band, and I am not putting a second number next to it.)

**2. Eight simultaneous changes make all eight unreadable.** Your own app
promised each volume push its own conversion verdict and "two weeks of data
before this muscle is revisited." Your next upper day now carries seven changes
at once — a fourth press set, a fourth rear-delt set, a fourth triceps set, a
fourth biceps set, a third rows set, the press 250 ownership repeat, and the
rows hold release. When two of those read "did not convert" in a fortnight,
neither of us will be able to say why. Worth knowing: Friday's three additions
had a real argument behind them — hams at 4, chest at 6, rear delt at 6 were all
at or under the growth floor. This morning's five don't. Quads, abs and biceps
were already at 10 weekly sets, triceps at 9, back at 8, and the best available
dose-response work puts the high-return tier at 5–10 sets and only the
intermediate tier at 11–18 (Pelland 2025, 67 studies). Friday's climbed *to* the
floor. Today's climbed *past* the best part of the curve.

**3. Two nights are dark and the flag will lie about it.** Sleep stops at 8/6
(7.75 h). 8/7 and 8/8 never got logged. Your eleven-night average is 6.8 h and
the seven-night debt is 1.8 h against the 7.5 you set. The catch is mechanical:
the debt flag is a three-night calendar mean, and when three consecutive nights
aren't on file it returns **clean**. So an unlogged night doesn't make the app
cautious, it makes it confident — and your next upper day carries the press 250
ownership repeat, which only banks on a genuinely clean day.

## Is lean safe?

Yes, and it is the least of your worries right now. Protein has been 100% of
target for eight straight weeks and is sitting at 190–200 g against a 146–196
window. Loads are being defended and then some — extension 155 is owned, press
250 has put up 8,8,7 twice, calves are holding 320. At 0.59%/wk you are inside
the range where a trained lifter keeps lean tissue, and this week you're barely
in a deficit at all. If anything the risk has flipped: the thing to watch is no
longer losing lean too fast, it's spending recovery you haven't logged on volume
you didn't need.

## The one move

**Hand back this morning's five volume additions and keep Friday's three.** Each
one has a one-tap undo already on file. Friday's three earn their place on the
floor argument; this morning's five don't, and they're the ones stacking seven
changes into a single upper day. Then have the actual conversation with your
coach — not about sets, about the block: if you're running a recomp, run it on
purpose, and let the volume go up in a shape where you can read what it bought.

## The horizon

Today's 164.5 is not information — Saturday was a high-sodium day and your own
measured water sensitivity is +1.11 lb the morning after one. Trend 163.37 plus
1.11 is 164.5, to the ounce. Expect it back near 163 within two or three clean
mornings.

Beyond that, the honest horizon is flat, not down, for as long as intake sits at
maintenance. If you want the trend moving again the lever is intake, not steps
(14,250 a day is plenty) and not the scale. If you're happy with flat, say so
and let's call it what it is. Either answer is fine. Drifting between the two is
the only one that isn't.

Three things to watch this week: whether the reads resume in the morning window
(one usable read out of the last three mornings), whether the nights get logged,
and whether the next upper day runs seven changes or two.
