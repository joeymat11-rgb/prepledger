The main problem is that several estimates and policy choices become claims about an individual's fat, muscle or recovery. The evidence supports training, adequate nutrition and sleep; it does not establish the app's exact personal thresholds or guarantees.

- **Body composition / calorie changes (S01/S04/S08):** scale and strength trends do not identify fat loss, muscle retention or exhausted fat loss. Yet `energyBalanceTarget` uses that interpretation in calorie instructions and the volume proposal's growth gate. A single DEXA also does not measure the partition of subsequent weight change. Preserve the recorded observations; present the interpretation as a hypothesis. Changing the calorie/volume decision rule needs an explicit owner decision.
- **Maintenance and EA (S01/S02):** matched intake/weight dates are a good correction, but the step baseline uses a different date selection and affects the calorie band. The estimate's interval omits intake and tissue-partition uncertainty. The 25 kcal/kg FFM boundary may remain a named precautionary policy; it cannot establish muscle protection or clearance. Existing D11 and D15 arithmetic defects remain separate known issues, not new science discoveries.
- **Protein (S03):** 2.5 g/kg FFM is a defensible provisional target in a relevant deficit context, conditional on a usable FFM estimate. It is not a universal minimum or measured individual requirement. Current engine explanations and coach instructions overstate that distinction.
- **Volume (S05):** fractional indirect sets are a supported counting convention. Pelland's 2.05% SDES is variability across research studies, not Joe's or Dad's detectable benefit. The app turns it into a required set increment and an individual expected-gain figure in a proposal. That inference needs correction; the size and timing of a replacement increment are owner choices.
- **Effort and sleep (S06/S07):** exact RIR-to-rep steps are policies. `rirPlan` defaults every final set to failure, while its later comment describes 1–2 RIR with occasional failure. The 2026 ACSM guidance does not require failure. Today promises walking without lean-mass/recovery cost and applies a small sleep trial directly to this person's food-versus-sleep choice. Those claims exceed the evidence. Missing-sleep protections already improved in A must remain intact.
- **Breaks and refeeds (S09):** retain the dated refeed retirement and symptom-based proposal safeguards. Replace absolute metabolic claims with uncertainty. ICECAP found appetite benefits without detected between-group body-composition advantages. The refeed reanalysis challenged FFM/RMR conclusions but retained a dry-FFM difference; it did not establish that refeeds can never help.

Several old findings are already corrected: the monthly gain-rate unit, 12.2% body-fat protein switch, selected-max progression anchor, missing old-record variance term, and automatic EA-number-to-diet-break inference. Do not restore them or report them as new failures. Native progression explanations also describe the current rule more honestly than the legacy text.

Do not infer Dad's profile from his relationship to Joe. Setup's refusal to invent a working load or nutrition target is useful.
