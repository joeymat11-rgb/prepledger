# Independent sources and actual access

Searched 2026-09-13 America/New_York, continuing into 2026-09-14 UTC. Primary articles, original reviews and official guidelines only support conclusions. Publisher-indexed passages are distinguished from directly opened full text. A full text being available does not mean every page or supplement was read. No article PDFs, full copyrighted texts or participant datasets are republished here. Numbers describe study populations, never current Joe/Dad measurements.

## R01 — Hall, energy density

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17848938/); DOI 10.1038/sj.ijo.0803720. Online 2007-09-11; issue 2008-03. Mathematical body-composition model: energy per unit weight change depends on initial composition and loss magnitude. It does not identify this athlete's partition. Abstract read; Nature/PMC full-text attempts failed or returned browser checks. No full-text/supplement verification claimed.

## R02 — Careau et al., energy compensation

[Author-hosted original paper](https://gurven.anth.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/sitefiles/papers/careauetal2021.pdf); DOI 10.1016/j.cub.2021.08.016; 2021-10-25 issue. Full paper available; abstract, sample/method passages and within-person analysis read. Main observational sample 1,754 adults, mean compensation about 28%; longitudinal component 68 adults aged 70–90 observed twice seven years apart. Neither estimates a particular person's response to prescribed walking. Supplement/table S2 not read. Correction to my initial framing: this is not exclusively cross-sectional.

## R03 — Murphy and Koehler, energy deficit with resistance training

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623696/); DOI 10.1111/sms.14075. Online 2021-10-13; 2022-01 issue. Meta-analysis: lean-mass effect -0.57 (p=.02); strength comparison -0.31 (p=.28). The approximately 500-kcal regression boundary concerns prevented gains, not an individual loss cliff. Abstract read. Author PDF exceeded access tool size limit; full methods/age distribution and supplements unverified. Nonsignificance is not equivalence.

## R04 — Garthe et al., slower versus faster loss

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21558571/); DOI 10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97; 2011-04 issue. Twenty-four athletes, assigned 0.7/1.4% weekly loss, achieved approximately 0.7/1.0%; intervention durations differed. Lean body mass changed +2.1% versus -0.2%. Small study supports caution, not an exact personal optimum. Abstract only; original full text/supplements unverified. This agrees with the later N1 correction, not the older assigned-rate interpretation.

## R05 — IOC REDs consensus and correction

[Official consensus](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994), 2023; [correction metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38325885/), 2024-02-07, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994corr1. Publisher-indexed primary passages on male EA, multifactorial assessment and prolonged/severe exposure were read; direct pages failed. No universal male 25 threshold established. Correction notice identifies updated Figure 6; the revised figure and supplementary diagnostic material were not inspected. This is partial consensus access, not verification of the whole clinical instrument.

## R06 — Guisado-Cuadrado et al., short-term low EA

[Institutional original paper](https://burjcdigital.urjc.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/c70d95f2-92ab-4e2a-8bfd-329957732f5c/content); DOI 10.1111/sms.70249; accepted 2026-02-20, March issue. Abstract/methods and selected tables read from full PDF. Thirteen studies, 145 women and men, 2–14 days, heterogeneous exposures/outcomes; no pooled meta-analysis. This is not a male-specific validation of 25 or a personal clearance threshold. Later discussion and external supplements not fully checked.

## R07 — Refalo, Trexler and Helms, protein during restriction

[Author/institution paper](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/bitstreams/3480bb69-c09c-4e5c-814e-5c232ea07e27/download); DOI 10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888; [issue metadata](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1665427): 2026-08-01, 48(4):398–412; online work circulated in 2025, exact online date not verified. Selected methods, results, practical interpretation and limitations read. Twenty-nine studies, 729 participants; only five experimentally compared protein intakes. Exploratory slopes: body-mass basis .07 (95% HDI -.01,.14), FFM .06 (.01,.12). Predominantly young/non-obese trained adults in deficits. OSF supplement access failed; no outlier reanalysis claimed. The practical FFM recommendation assumes reasonable measurement accuracy.

## R08 — Helms et al., surplus trial

[Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620361/); DOI 10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y; 2023-11-02. Publisher/PMC-indexed abstract and key passages read; direct access later returned a browser check. Twenty-one randomized, 17 completed eight weeks: maintenance versus 5%/15% surpluses. Greater gain was more clearly associated with skinfold increases than broad additional muscle/strength benefits. Small sample and short follow-up; not validation of a precise monthly gain cap. Full tables/supplements unverified.

## R09 — Pelland et al., training dose-response

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/); [original final paper mirror](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf); DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w. Online 2025-12-04; 2026-02 issue. Methods §2.6, tables/tiers and limitations §4.6 read. Predominantly young adults; fractional set models favored. Hypertrophy slope .24 percentage points/set (95% CrI .15,.33); considerable unexplained variation. SDES 2.05% derives from non-training control research, not personal measurement error. Main paper is final, not merely its preprint. OSF supplement access failed; no complete correction/retraction search guarantee.

## R10 — Roth et al., training volume during restriction

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36114738/); DOI 10.1111/sms.14237. Online 2022-10-05; 2023-01 issue. Thirty-eight trained men, six weeks at total intake 30 kcal/kg with 2.8 g protein/kg FFM; five versus three sets. Lean-mass changes -0.51±2.30 versus -0.92±1.59 kg; between-group p=.966. This is not evidence of identical preservation or proof of a minimum safe set dose. Abstract only; full-text tables/supplements unverified.

## R11 — Bickel et al., reduced maintenance dose

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21131862/); DOI 10.1249/MSS.0b013e318207c15d; 2011 publication. Seventy adults aged 20–35 or 60–75, 16 weeks training followed by 32 weeks with reduced dosing/detraining. Reduced dosing retained hypertrophy better in younger adults than older adults. Not a trial of trained people dieting. Abstract read; full text/supplement not read. Neither participant's age is inferred from this study.

## R12 — Halperin et al., RIR prediction accuracy

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/); DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x; online 2021, 2022 issue. Meta-analysis, 414 participants: mean underprediction .95 repetitions (95% CI .17,1.73), I²97.9%; accuracy improved nearer failure. Supports uncertain effort estimates, not an exact next-session dose table. Abstract read; full methods/supplements not checked. Not evidence that all individual errors equal one rep.

## R13 — ACSM 2026 resistance-training overview

[Official position stand/full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/); DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897. Online 2026-03-05; April issue. Overview of 137 reviews and more than 30,000 healthy adults. Selected failure/effort sections read: failure is not obligatory, and evidence does not establish exact RIR targets. Broad guideline, not a deficit-specific personal prescription. Full reference corpus/supplements not audited. It updates the 2009 position; the app's precise bins are not thereby validated.

## R14 — Mitter et al., repeat bench-press reliability

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35511896/); [original paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0268074); DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0268074; 2022-05. Abstract read and full paper located; detailed methods/supplement not fully read. Twenty-four trained women and men repeated bench testing one week apart; reported SEM about .7–1.1 repetitions across loads. Not a validation of selected multi-set records or independent within-session errors. Correct the older corpus's men-only description.

## R15 — Craven et al., acute sleep loss

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35708888/); DOI 10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y. Online 2022-06-16; November issue. Abstract and primary-indexed strength section read. Overall review 69 studies/959 participants; strength subset mean effect -2.85% (95% CI -4.47,-1.23), heterogeneity present. Acute sleep-loss protocols do not validate the app's exact 6.5/7-hour rules. Full paper direct access failed; supplements unverified. Comparing a group effect to individual repeat error cannot establish irrelevance.

## R16 — Nedeltcheva et al., sleep opportunity during dieting

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921542/); DOI 10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006; 2010-10-05. Ten overweight adults, 14-day crossover, 5.5 versus 8.5 hours time in bed. Shorter opportunity produced less fat loss and more FFM loss. Small, acute study outside trained-athlete context; it cannot promise that a particular full night makes all loss fat. Abstract only; full protocol/supplements not read.

## R17 — Coleman et al., planned deload

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/); DOI 10.7717/peerj.16777; 2024-01-22. Thirty-nine young adults, nine-week programme, one week of training cessation versus continuous training. Lower-body strength favored continuous training; no appreciable hypertrophy difference detected. Not a symptom-guided reduction or 5% load reset. Abstract read, full paper/supplement not read. Nonsignificance does not prove equivalent responses.

## R18 — Nait-Yahia et al., deficit/volume trial

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42103927/); DOI 10.1038/s41430-026-01757-8. Online 2026-05-08; August issue. Sixteen trained adults, four-week 40% restriction, 2.3 g/kg body-mass protein, 12 versus 30 sets/muscle/week. Strength advantages for higher volume without detected FFM treatment effects. Abstract read, selected publisher methods indexed; full text/supplement not obtained. [Publisher Crossmark](https://crossmark.crossref.org/dialog/?doi=10.1038%2Fs41430-026-01757-8) indicated current record. This neither proves FFM loss nor makes strength a tissue measurement.

## R19 — ICECAP randomized trial

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587549/); DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002636; 2021. Sixty-one trained adults, 12 restriction weeks; intermittent arm inserted three maintenance weeks. No detected between-group advantage for fat/FFM/strength/resting expenditure; appetite ratings improved. Abstract only. Different elapsed duration and sample uncertainty matter. Appetite/tolerability is not proof of better adherence, and nonsignificance is not proof of no metabolic effect.

## R20 — ICECAP one-week secondary analysis

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33630880/); DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0247292; 2021-02-25. Twenty-six trained adults, pre/post maintenance week after restriction: body weight/FFM and resting expenditure increased transiently, appetite improved. No concurrent control for this comparison. Abstract only; full measurement protocol/supplements unverified. Relevant contrary evidence to an absolute zero-effect statement, not proof of durable metabolic repair.

## R21 — Poon et al., breaks review and corrected figure

[Primary review abstract](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article-abstract/83/1/59/7513121); DOI 10.1093/nutrit/nuad168; online 2024, 2025 issue. Twelve RCTs/881 people, mixed populations. [Correction](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/83/1/200/7616492), 2024-02-29, DOI 10.1093/nutrit/nuae018: Figure 4 mistakenly showed the trained subgroup; corrected overall RMR mean difference -47.31 kcal/day (95% CI -73.51,-21.11). Abstract/correction text read. Full subgroup interval/supplements not independently verified; older corpus intervals disagree, so no trained-specific numeric conclusion is asserted.

## R22 — Campbell et al., refeed trial

[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33467235/); DOI 10.3390/jfmk5010019; 2020-03-08. Twenty-seven completers, seven weeks, approximately 25% restriction with/without two consecutive carbohydrate refeeds. Authors claimed FFM/RMR preservation. Abstract and selected publisher passages read, not full original supplement. Reanalysis and response below materially qualify that conclusion. Article version notes show later updates; these were not audited as a complete version diff.

## R23 — Peos et al., reanalysis

[Original comment PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jfmk/jfmk-05-00085/article_deploy/jfmk-05-00085-v2.pdf); DOI 10.3390/jfmk5040085; 2020-11-20. Four-page text read. Challenged nominal-significance and completer-only interpretations; FFM/RMR did not differ significantly, while dry-FFM difference persisted. Simplistic baseline-carried-forward analysis estimated .9 kg (95% CI .33,1.46) dry-FFM difference. That imputation has limitations. Linked Zenodo calculations were not rerun/read; no independent statistical replication claimed. It does not support a blanket no-benefit assertion.

## R24 — Campbell response

[Original response PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jfmk/jfmk-05-00086/article_deploy/jfmk-05-00086.pdf); DOI 10.3390/jfmk5040086; 2020-11-24. Opening and statistical response on pages 1–2 read; later pages not fully inspected. Agrees on significance pattern, retains interpretation using effect estimates, and reports failure to establish equivalence. This is relevant conflicting interpretation, not a new trial or confirmation of a benefit. No equivalence analysis independently reproduced.

## R25 — Da Silva et al., reverse dieting preliminary analysis

[Primary publisher abstract/PDF](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15502783.2025.2550185); DOI 10.1080/15502783.2025.2550185; 2025, journal 22 supplement 2 (exact day not independently confirmed). Publisher-indexed complete short abstract read. Forty-nine trained adults randomized after 5% weight loss, 15-week follow-up; regain comparison p=.053. This is preliminary abstract-level reporting, not a fully reported definitive trial or equivalence result. It makes a categorical claim that no controlled evidence exists outdated; it does not prove one exit strategy optimal.

## Additional source screening and limits

- Espinar 2026, DOI [10.3390/nu18030379](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/3/379), published 2026-01-23: publisher-indexed header/abstract identify a narrative review, not a newly performed 32-to-20 EA experiment. That exact underlying comparison was not verified. Keep it unverified rather than turn the review into a trial.
- Correction searches for Refalo and Pelland found no specific notice in returned results; this is not an exhaustive retraction clearance. IOC and Poon corrections were positively identified. Nait-Yahia currentness was checked through Crossmark.
- Unavailable OSF supplements, publisher errors/429 responses, PMC browser checks, the oversized Murphy PDF and uninspected raw data limit certainty. No access workaround, account, payment or private-data upload was used. Discoveries on blogs, Reddit, commercial commentary, OpenAlex and ResearchGate were not final supporting evidence.
- Source access improved during the audit: the final Pelland paper replaced preprint-only access; Nait-Yahia's abstract replaced title-only access; Peos's full comment and Campbell's reply replaced an unverified historical assertion; Careau's longitudinal component corrected an initially overbroad cross-sectional description. These changes are retained rather than hidden.
