# Science audit source register

Owner356 • E author • search cutoff: 2026-09-13 America/New_York (through 2026-09-14 UTC). Source checks used ordinary public web access. These are the sources actually used, not a systematic or exhaustive search of all training and nutrition literature.

Access terminology: “selected full text” means the original article was accessible and the named sections were read, not that every page or supplement was reviewed. “Original indexed text” is text of the original publication returned by the web index; it is distinguished from directly retrieved full text. Secondary blog/search summaries are not evidence here. Inaccessible methods, supplements and corrections remain unverified. No article PDF or participant dataset is copied into this package.

## R01 — Refalo, Trexler and Helms: protein during energy restriction

2026 Aug issue, published online in 2025. DOI [10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888](https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888). [Final author record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1665427); [author full text](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/bitstreams/3480bb69-c09c-4e5c-814e-5c232ea07e27/download). Access: selected full text, methods/result/practical-application sections; no separate supplement verified.

Exploratory review/meta-regression of 29 studies in non-obese adults with resistance-training experience during restriction. The FFM-scaled protein slope was positive but heterogeneous. Approximately 2.5 g/kg FFM is an average model crossing, not an individually established minimum or optimum. Higher doses may help some people; food trade-offs remain. Final authorship resolves the repository's “TBC”; this does not validate its precise personal floor.

## R02 — Pelland et al.: training volume/frequency dose response

Online 2025-12-04; 2026 Feb issue. DOI [10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w). [Published article PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf). Access: selected full text, SDES methods, dose models/Table 3 and limitations. Linked OSF material was not successfully verified.

67 studies overall; hypertrophy subset 35 studies/1,032 participants. Fractional indirect-set counting improved model fit; average gains showed diminishing returns. The 2.05% SDES derives from external untrained non-training control variability. It contextualizes group effects, not a validated personal dose threshold. The paper's fitted average slope does not establish Earned's individual square-root gain prediction.

## R03 — Garthe et al.: slower/faster weight loss

2011 May. DOI [10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97). [Original published article](https://www.paulogentil.com/pdf/Effect%20of%20Two%20Different%20Weight-Loss%20Rates%20on%20Body%20Composition%20and%20Strength%20and%20Power-Related%20Performance%20in%20Elite%20Athletes.pdf). Access: selected full text, design/results; supplements not reviewed.

24 athletes, with different diet durations; achieved losses averaged about 0.7% versus 1.0% body mass/week. Slower loss favored lean-mass outcomes. The repository's male subgroup changes (+1.7%/−2.0%) are real subgroup figures, not the whole sample. This small intervention supports caution with faster loss, not an exact optimum for every user.

## R04 — Murphy and Koehler: energy deficit and training outcomes

Online 2021-10-08; 2022 Jan issue. DOI [10.1111/sms.14075](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.14075). [Author manuscript](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1654777/document.pdf). Access: selected full text, abstract/meta-regression/discussion; supplementary dataset not independently analyzed.

Meta-analysis found impaired lean-mass gains with energy deficiency; the strength comparison was inconclusive. The approximately 500 kcal/day crossing is a group model estimate for absent gains, not a universal boundary between muscle preservation and loss. Preserved strength cannot establish preserved muscle. Neither an individual fat-loss fraction nor the cause of one person's stalled lifts is identified.

## R05 — Roth et al.: 12 versus 20 sets during restriction

Online 2022-10-05; 2023 Jan issue. DOI [10.1111/sms.14237](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.14237). Access: selected full text, methods/results/limitations; supplementary Table S2 not read.

38 trained men, six weeks; energy intake was 30 kcal/kg body mass/day, not a deficit of that size; protein 2.8 g/kg FFM/day. Comparing 12 versus 20 quadriceps sets, BIA lean-mass changes were −0.51 ± 2.30 versus −0.92 ± 1.59 kg, interaction p=.785. This was not an equivalence trial or a test of 2–5 weekly sets.

## R06 — IOC REDs consensus and correction

2023-09-26 online. DOI [10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994). Access: original publisher-indexed male/threshold/conceptual-model text; direct site blocked and alternate PDF retrieval failed. [2024 correction](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/58/3/e4), DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994corr1: publisher notice read; Figure 6 **and supplementary file 5** were updated. Their corrected contents were not verified.

Consensus explicitly treats male thresholds as uncertain and variable, not universal safety clearance. Modeled EA is one contextual input; diagnosis needs broader clinical assessment. This audit does not validate the corrected traffic-light tool or reproduce it.

## R07 — Espinar et al.: energy-availability review

2026-01-23. DOI [10.3390/nu18030379](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/3/379). Access: original indexed narrative-review text; direct publisher/PMC retrieval unsuccessful.

A narrative review, not the experiment represented in Earned's receipt. Its discussion of changing expenditure definitions relies on other work, including R08. Do not attribute a new measured 32-to-20 EA experiment or demonstrated physiological equivalence to Espinar.

## R08 — Taguchi and Manore: EA assessment opinion

2022-10-24. DOI [10.3389/fspor.2022.885631](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2022.885631/full). Access: selected full text, definitions and male soccer example.

An opinion article recalculates a cited male soccer sample from 31.9 to 19.7 kcal/kg FFM/day using another expenditure convention, and discusses associated biological markers. It is not a randomized physiological-change experiment. Deliberate walking can enter some exercise definitions; subtracting “all walking” and transporting a cutoff between methods is not justified. The cited soccer participant data were not independently reanalyzed.

## R09 — Hall: required energy deficit per unit weight loss

Online 2007-09-11; 2008 Mar issue. DOI [10.1038/sj.ijo.0803720](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.ijo.0803720). [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17848938/). Access: abstract and original indexed energy-density text; full article/supplement not independently retrieved.

Modeling explains why weight-loss energy density varies with body composition and partitioning. It does not validate 3,800 kcal/lb as a measured individual constant. A prior can be useful while its uncertainty and assumptions remain visible; arithmetic based on that prior is not calorimetry.

## R10 — Careau et al.: energy compensation

2021-10-25 issue. DOI [10.1016/j.cub.2021.08.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.08.016). [Author-hosted published article](https://gurven.anth.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/sitefiles/papers/careauetal2021.pdf). Access: abstract/introduction and design description; individual dataset not analyzed.

Observational analysis of 1,754 adults estimates roughly 28% average compensation, with variation. The association does not identify the causal response to adding Joe's or Dad's next steps. It cannot establish a personal 25–30% interval or a guarantee that more walking adds deficit without recovery/body-composition cost.

## R11 — ACSM resistance-training prescription overview

2026 publication. DOI [10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897). [Original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/). Access: abstract and selected recommendations/discussion; online supplement not read.

Overview of 137 systematic reviews in healthy adults. Supports progressive, feasible resistance training with flexible prescriptions. Failure is not required; exact optimal RIR remains inadequately established. It provides no validation of Earned's fixed +1/+2/+3 rep algorithm, personal set optimum, universal four-day schedule or readiness score. This is the current guidance checked, rather than relying exclusively on the 2009 stand.

## R12 — Helms et al.: RPE versus percentage loading

2018. DOI [10.3389/fphys.2018.00247](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2018.00247/full). Access: abstract, Table 4 and selected discussion; no separate supplement read.

21 trained men over eight weeks. The combined strength estimate favoring RPE was ES 0.48 ± 0.68 (90% limits), p=.38. The result is compatible with substantial uncertainty; it is not decisive superiority. The protocol does not test Earned's exact terminal-RIR-to-next-reps mapping.

## R13 — Nuzzo et al.: repetitions versus percentage 1RM

Online 2023-10-04; 2024 Feb issue. DOI [10.1007/s40279-023-01937-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01937-7). [Published article](https://www.fitasaphysio.com/uploads/4/3/3/4/43345381/s40279-023-01937-7.pdf). Access: abstract, model description and selected limitations; supplements not read.

Meta-regression of 952 tests/7,289 people, predominantly healthy young adults, with substantial exercise variation. Its fitted relationships do not establish a personal Epley error of 0.31 reps or the repository's historical Brzycki comparison. The prior progression audit already recognizes those attribution errors.

## R14 — Mitter et al.: repetitions-to-failure reliability

2022-05-05. DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0268074](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0268074). [Published PDF](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0268074&type=printable). Access: abstract/methods; supporting data not reanalyzed.

24 trained adults, repeat bench-press tests at 70/80/90% of current 1RM; SEM approximately 0.7–1.1 repetitions. Useful context, not direct validation of every exercise, absolute-load work-set covariance or Earned's record-classification false-positive rate. Relative test intensity could require different absolute loads between visits.

## R15 — Halperin et al.: RIR prediction accuracy

Online 2021-09-20; 2022 Feb issue. DOI [10.1007/s40279-021-01559-x](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/). Access: original abstract; author manuscript located but not relied on beyond retrieved excerpts.

Exploratory meta-analysis: 12 studies/414 participants, average underprediction 0.95 reps (95% CI 0.17–1.73), very high heterogeneity. Nearer-failure predictions tended to improve. This supports acknowledging error and allowing “unsure”; it does not prove that every reported value ≥3 warrants the same precise progression.

## R16 — Craven et al.: acute sleep loss and performance

2022-06-16. DOI [10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y). [Published article](https://d-nb.info/126507996X/34). Access: abstract, Table 2, strength-endurance/sensitivity discussion; separate supplement not read.

69 studies/959 participants. Strength −2.85% (95% CI −4.47 to −1.23); strength-endurance −9.85% (−19.60 to −0.13), with much thinner, sensitivity-unstable evidence. These categories and units differ from work-set rep SEM. Results do not derive the exact 6.5/7-hour rules or a personal automatic load reduction.

## R17 — Nedeltcheva et al.: sleep opportunity during dieting

2010-10-05. DOI [10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006). [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921542/). Access: abstract; published PDF located but not fully reviewed.

Ten overweight adults underwent controlled 14-day diet periods with 5.5 versus 8.5 hours in bed. Shorter sleep opportunity changed the composition of lost weight. This does not quantify muscle loss from one bad night in a trained user or guarantee that a full night makes subsequent loss exclusively fat.

## R18 — Coleman et al.: planned complete deload

2024-01-22. DOI [10.7717/peerj.16777](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.16777). [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/). Access: original abstract/design only; no effect-size conclusion used.

39 young adults in nine weeks of high-volume training; the intervention was one week of complete cessation. It does not test Earned's reactive stall reset or establish an optimal deload trigger. That distinction prevents using adjacent research as direct algorithm validation.

## R19 — Bickel et al.: reduced training to retain adaptations

2011. DOI [10.1249/MSS.0b013e318207c15d](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e318207c15d). [Published article](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Exercise%20Dosing%20to%20Retain%20Resistance%20Training%20Adaptations%20in%20Young%20and%20Older%20Adults.pdf). Access: abstract/methods; no participant-data analysis.

70 initially recruited adults, 56 entering maintenance after training; lower-limb detraining/reduced-dose design. Very low doses retained size in younger but not older participants under the studied conditions. It is not a trained-dieter trial. Relationship labels give no basis for placing Dad into either age group.

## R20 — Helms et al.: energy surplus size

2023-11-02. DOI [10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y). Access: selected full text, abstract/results/limitations.

21 randomized, 17 completed; trained adults, eight weeks, maintenance versus approximately 5%/15% surplus prescriptions. Faster gain mainly tracked increased skinfolds, with uncertain muscle findings; bench strength favored the higher surplus in a contrary result. Small sample and imperfect separation limit inference. This supports cautious surplus choices, not a precise biological monthly muscle ceiling or “surplus can never help.”

## R21 — Peos et al.: ICECAP diet breaks

2021-08-01. DOI [10.1249/MSS.0000000000002636](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002636). [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587549/); [author manuscript](https://api.research-repository.uwa.edu.au/ws/files/123366957/Peos_Medicine_and_Science_in_Sports_and_Exercise_2021_Accepted.pdf). Access: abstract; manuscript located, not comprehensively read.

61 resistance-trained adults; continuous restriction versus breaks extending calendar time. No clear between-group body-composition or resting-expenditure advantage; some appetite measures favored breaks. Appetite findings are not guaranteed long-term adherence, nor proof of metabolic restoration at exactly 14 days.

## R22 — Siedler et al.: intermittent restriction in trained women

2023. DOI [10.5114/jhk/159960](https://doi.org/10.5114/jhk/159960). [Original article record/text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10170537/). Access: original indexed abstract/body excerpts; full PDF not reviewed.

Small study in resistance-trained women with diet breaks extending calendar duration. No clear body-composition/resting-metabolic advantage emerged. This adds a population contrast; it does not prove equivalence or directly establish a rule for either male user.

## R23 — Poon et al.: diet-break review

Online 2024; 2025 Jan issue. DOI [10.1093/nutrit/nuad168](https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article-abstract/83/1/59/7513121). Access: original abstract and indexed original Table 4; full article/supplement not independently reviewed.

12 RCTs/881 participants, heterogeneous interventions/populations. In three resistance-trained RMR studies the table's signed difference is −10.80 kcal/day (95% CI −67.41 to 45.82); reversing direction yields +10.80 (−45.82 to 67.41). It is uncertain, not evidence of exactly zero physiological effect. Population and coding direction must accompany the number.

## R24 — Campbell trial and Peos statistical comment

[Campbell 2020-03-08](https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5142/5/1/19), DOI 10.3390/jfmk5010019: original indexed abstract/discussion. [Peos 2020-11-20 comment](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/jfmk/jfmk-05-00085/article_deploy/jfmk-05-00085-v2.pdf?version=1606199050), DOI 10.3390/jfmk5040085: full substantive comment pp1–3 read; linked reanalysis archive not executed.

Two-day refeeds versus continuous restriction, 27 completers from 54 baseline participants. The comment rejects FFM/RMR superiority inferred from within-group significance, but dry-FFM differences persist under its analyses. This is a statistical correction argument, not a retraction or erasure of all favorable findings. No verified author reply or formal retraction was located in the targeted search; that is not an exhaustive absence claim.

## R25 — Da Silva et al.: preliminary reverse-dieting comparison

2025-08-26. DOI [10.1080/15502783.2025.2550185](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15502783.2025.2550185). Access: original journal supplement abstract text; full methods not available in the retrieved item.

49 trained adults randomized after weight loss to gradual reverse dieting, estimated maintenance or ad-libitum eating. Fifteen-week regain comparisons were inconclusive (p=.053). This updates the absolute “no RCT” statement, but a preliminary abstract cannot establish equivalence, exact maintenance accuracy or metabolic repair.

## R26 — Morton et al.: protein supplementation with training

Online 2017-07-11; 2018 Mar issue. DOI [10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/). [Published article](https://breathe-edu-downloads.s3.amazonaws.com/Morton-2018.pdf). Access: abstract/methods and original indexed breakpoint-results text; supplement not read.

49 studies/1,863 healthy adults. Supplementation added mean FFM 0.30 kg (95% CI 0.09–0.52). Estimated breakpoint 1.62 g/kg body mass/day had broad uncertainty (1.03–2.20). That model is not a universal maximum or the same evidence base as energy-restricted trained adults. Age moderation does not supply an individual prescription.

## R27 — Farley et al.: body-composition measurement precision

Online 2020-11-12; 2021 Jan issue. DOI [10.1123/ijsnem.2020-0061](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2020-0061). [Author repository abstract](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1256019/short-term-precision-error-of-body-composition-assessment-methods-in-resistance-trained-male-athletes). Access: original author abstract; full paper/supplement not reviewed.

32 resistance-trained men, repeated measurements across two days. Consecutive-day DXA least-significant changes exceeded same-day values, including approximately 1.97 kg for FFM. Precision is not accuracy; neither this result nor one scan establishes a universal ±1 percentage-point personal interval or the fat fraction of subsequent weight change.

## R28 — IOC REDs CAT2 rationale/validation companion

2023. DOI [10.1136/bjsports-2023-106914](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106914). Access: original publisher-indexed clinical/indicator discussion; direct full text blocked.

The authors discuss libido/morning-function changes as emerging male indicators while noting the lack of a validated tool defining normal versus risk for those outcomes. This supports cautious contextual questioning, not a diagnostic single-question classifier. No corrected CAT2 scoring implementation was independently validated in this audit.

## Material limits and unresolved source checks

The most consequential source-to-rule gaps above do not depend on an unread supplement: R02's SDES definition, R05's dose/outcome, R12's uncertainty and R16's outcome categories are in retrieved article text. Still, independent review must retain the limits above. R06's corrected Figure 6/supplement 5, R02's OSF material, R01's detailed extraction/supplements and the source data behind R14/R24 were not independently checked or reanalyzed. A complete current personalized model, clinical assessment and exhaustive correction/retraction search are outside this finite audit.

Targeted searches checked newer publications and correction/comment records through the cutoff. R01 final publication, R11's 2026 guidance, R25's 2025 preliminary trial and the R06/R24 correction history materially updated interpretation. No claim that every source is uncorrected, unretracted or fully verified is made.
