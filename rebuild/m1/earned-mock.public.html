Five minutes with Dad

Hand him the phone on the Morning scenario. Say nothing except the task line. Do not help. Write down where he pauses, what he taps that does nothing, and any word he asks about. Every number on the screens is sample data, not his.

The five tasks — read each one aloud, then wait

  1. “It’s morning. You just stepped on the scale and it said 180.7. Put that in.”
  2. “What should you eat today?” (he should answer from the screen, not scroll)
  3. “Why is it telling you that?” (watch whether he finds “Why this plan?” without a hint)
  4. “You’re at the gym. Do the exercise it shows and record what you did — say you got 9, then 8, then 7, then 6.” (the reserve question after the first set: does he understand “clean reps left”?)
  5. “It’s offering you a heavier weight. Decide.” (then: “Undo that.”)

What to watch

Weigh-inDoes he find the button, type the number, and trust that it saved? (No confirmation screen is deliberate.)
InstructionDoes “Eat about 2,300 kcal” read as the answer, or does he hunt for more?
WhyDoes he read it, or bounce? Which sentence does he stop on?
SetsStepper vs typing: does he know reps are counted with + and −? Does “clean reps left” need a definition?
DecisionTwo plain choices, nothing pre-selected. Does he ask “what should I pick?” — that is a design finding, not a failure.
WordsList every word he asks about. Those words get demoted or renamed, never explained with a tooltip.

Afterwards — three questions, his words verbatim

  1. “What does this app do?”
  2. “What would you do tomorrow morning?”
  3. “What confused you?”

Bring the notes back to the build chat as they are. No summary, no fixing on the spot.
